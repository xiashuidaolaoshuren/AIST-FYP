# Abstract and Introduction Draft

## Abstract

Large Language Models (LLMs) can generate fluent but factually incorrect statements, especially in Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) settings where responses are expected to be grounded in cited evidence. This project presents an end-to-end, lightweight framework for hallucination detection and mitigation in citation-centric question answering, with a focus on transparency and practical deployability. Instead of relying on a large, opaque LLM-as-a-Judge, we implement a trainless multi-signal verifier that combines four complementary signals: intrinsic uncertainty from token-level entropy, retrieval-grounded lexical and entity coverage, zero-shot Natural Language Inference (NLI) contradiction detection, and self-agreement across stochastic generations. These signals are fused by a rule-based aggregator to produce claim-level verdicts and confidence breakdowns. Based on verifier outputs, a mitigation orchestrator applies filtering, re-ranking, and selective re-prompting to reduce unsupported or contradictory claims before final response delivery. The system is evaluated with benchmarks tailored to RAG factuality and citation quality, including RAGTruth and CiteEval/CiteBench-style workflows, and is accompanied by a minimal confidence UI for interpretability. Results and analysis indicate that the multi-signal verifier provides exceptionally high recall for factual error detection (outperforming fine-tuned baselines like LettuceDetect in catching false negatives), making it highly suitable for safety-critical deployments. Furthermore, downstream mitigation, specifically NLI-guided filtering and reprompting, dramatically improves structural citation quality—elevating overall factual grounding from "Poor" to "Good" benchmark ratings. This demonstrates that a modular, trainless architecture can offer rigorous factuality safeguards and better transparency at a lower complexity, while preserving extensibility for future learned fusion components.

## Introduction

Recent advances in LLMs have significantly improved the quality of natural language generation, enabling strong performance in open-domain question answering, summarization, and conversational systems. However, a persistent limitation remains: LLMs may produce hallucinations, namely statements that are plausible in form but unsupported or incorrect in content. This issue becomes especially critical in high-stakes and evidence-sensitive applications, where users expect not only fluent answers but also reliable source attribution.

Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) is widely adopted to reduce hallucinations by grounding model outputs in retrieved documents. Nevertheless, retrieval alone does not guarantee factual correctness. Prior studies and benchmarks have shown that systems can still produce cited hallucinations, where references are present but fail to support the exact generated claim. This gap motivates the need for claim-level verification and corrective control, rather than treating citation presence as a proxy for truthfulness.

In this project, we design and implement a complete Generator-Retriever-Verifier-Mitigator pipeline that addresses this gap through a trainless verification strategy. Our verifier does not require task-specific training and instead aggregates multiple zero-shot signals that capture different dimensions of reliability: (1) intrinsic uncertainty estimated from generation-time token distributions, (2) retrieval-grounded heuristics such as entity/number coverage and lexical overlap, (3) zero-shot NLI-based contradiction and entailment probabilities, and (4) self-agreement under stochastic resampling. A transparent rule-based aggregator combines these signals to produce interpretable verdicts for each atomic claim.

Beyond detection, we emphasize mitigation as a first-class objective. Verified claim signals are consumed by an orchestration layer that can suppress contradictory content, flag low-confidence spans, re-rank evidence candidates, and trigger targeted re-prompting when necessary. This design supports practical deployment by balancing factuality improvements with controllable computational overhead. To support user trust and auditing, we also provide a minimal confidence interface that exposes signal-level evidence behind each verdict.

Our work contributes in three ways. First, we provide a modular, trainless verifier architecture tailored to citation-grounded RAG. Second, we operationalize a detection-to-mitigation workflow that closes the loop between factual diagnosis and response correction. Third, we establish an evaluation workflow over RAG-focused hallucination and citation benchmarks, enabling ablation and error analysis of each verifier signal. Collectively, these contributions demonstrate that robust factuality control can be achieved without depending on heavyweight judge models, while leaving clear upgrade paths to future learned fusion or domain-specific adaptation.
