### Key Insight on statement_rating Gap

**Our per-sentence citation quality is consistently better** (CE +1.047, CR-EditDist +0.455, CR-IterCoe +0.38) than LettuceDetect. The statement_rating inversion (0.593 vs 0.930) is a **CiteEval aggregation artifact**, not a reflection of citation quality:

- LettuceDetect: density=0.073 → 92.7% of sentences labeled Model-type (unrequired citations) → exempted from strict CiteEval rating → default 1.0 contribution
- Our Verifier: density=0.829 → 86.9% of sentences labeled Retrieval-type → all subjected to strict CiteEval rating
- **Theoretical parity ceiling**: Even if all our CR ratings reached 1.0, statement_rating ≈ 0.928 (at 86.9% Retrieval weight)

## Part 2: Analysis Summary

**Why statement_rating gap exists** (and why it's not about citation quality):
- CiteEval's Citation Attribution (CA) module labels sentences as Retrieval/Model/Response based on textual content, NOT citation presence
- LettuceDetect generates minimal cites (7.3% density) → 92.7% Model-labeled → exempt from strict rating → +1.0 per sentence
- Our verifier generates dense contextual support (83% density) → 87% Retrieval-labeled → all strictly rated → realistic scores
- **Verifier's per-sentence citation quality is superior on every individual metric** (CE, CR-IterCoe, CR-EditDist) but is penalized by CiteEval's aggregation mechanic
